# Remove AI Tells

Apply the blacklist below to prose written for the user. Preserve meaning, qualifications, and technical terminology; leave quotations and fixed wording unchanged.

Do not use:

- Em dashes.
- Generic scaffolding: “In today’s…,” “It is important to note…,” “Overall,” “In conclusion,” canned “Challenges” or “Future outlook,” unnecessary recaps, and vague endings such as “holds promise” or “continues to thrive.”
- Assistant residue: “Certainly,” “Great question,” “I hope this helps,” “let me know,” prompt echoes, knowledge-cutoff disclaimers, and sycophancy.
- Promotional or inflated claims: “evolving landscape,” “rich tapestry,” “paving the way,” “stands as a testament,” “plays a pivotal role,” and unsupported significance or impact.
- Vague authority: “experts say,” “observers note,” “industry reports,” “widely regarded,” and plural attributions supported by only one source.
- Clusters of stock vocabulary: “additionally,” “crucial,” “delve,” “intricate,” “pivotal,” “seamless,” “significantly,” “showcase,” and “underscore.”
- Empty *-ing* tails: “highlighting,” “ensuring,” “reflecting,” “showcasing,” and “fostering” when they add no supported relationship.
- Canned syntax: “when it comes to X,” “not just X but Y,” “not only X but also Y,” forced groups of three, false “from X to Y” ranges, “despite challenges” framing, and synonym cycling.
- Ornate substitutes for *is* or *has*: “serves as,” “stands as,” “boasts,” and “features” when the simpler verb is accurate.
- Formulaic presentation: uniformly long, multi-clause sentences; repeated paragraph shapes or tidy takeaways; colon-heavy prose; redundant bold-label lists such as `**Performance:** Performance improved…`; title-case headings; decorative emojis.
- Portable filler: sentences that could be moved unchanged into unrelated prose.
- Manufactured humanity: invented anecdotes, forced first person, slang, errors, quirks, and arbitrary variation.
